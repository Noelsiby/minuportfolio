Every subscription business has the same quiet problem: customers leave, and by the time anyone notices, it's too late to win them back. For my Customer Churn Prediction & Retention Intelligence project, I wanted to answer a practical question: **which customers are most likely to leave, and what can the business do about it?**

## The Data

I worked with **7,043 telecom customer records**, covering contract types, services, billing and whether each customer churned. Before any modelling, most of the work was the unglamorous part:

- Cleaning missing and inconsistent values with **Pandas**
- Exploratory data analysis (EDA) to see how churn varied across segments
- Feature engineering to turn raw columns into signals a model could learn from

## The Model

I compared two classification models in **Scikit-learn**:

- **Logistic Regression**, which is simple, fast and easy to explain to non-technical stakeholders
- **Random Forest**, which captures interactions between features that a linear model can miss

The final model reached **80% accuracy and 76% recall**. For churn, recall matters more than it looks: missing a customer who is about to leave costs far more than reaching out to one who would have stayed anyway.

## The Dashboard

A model on its own doesn't change decisions, so I built an interactive **Power BI** dashboard that tracks:

- **Churn risk** across customer segments
- **Contract mix**, to see which plans keep customers longest
- **Revenue exposure**, to see how much revenue is at risk if high-risk customers leave

This turns the model's predictions into something a retention team can actually act on, focusing campaigns on the high-value segments where keeping a customer matters most.

## What I Took Away

1. **Pick the metric that matches the business cost.** Here that was recall, not just accuracy.
2. **Explainability earns trust.** A slightly simpler model that stakeholders understand often gets used more.
3. **The dashboard is the product.** Insights only matter once someone can make a decision with them.
